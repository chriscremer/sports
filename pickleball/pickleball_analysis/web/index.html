How this reconstruction works METHOD & LIMITS ↓

Observed movement

YOLO11s-pose detects 17 body keypoints at 15 Hz. The overlay uses those image coordinates. Overlapping far-court crops recover small players. Court-side and image-box continuity assign local IDs. Gaps of at most 0.4 seconds between the same player’s observations can be interpolated: dashed in the overlay, translucent in 3D. Longer gaps stay empty; IDs are not named identities.

Estimated geometry

Four manually selected court corners map feet onto a 6.096 × 13.4112 m court. Skeletons are lifted onto an upright plane through each player’s feet. Focal length is assumed. Hidden limb depth and airborne feet are not recovered.

Learned ball tracking

TrackNet-Pickleball ↗ locates the ball using three consecutive RGB frames at the original video frame rate. Overlapping predictions are averaged; isolated jumps are rejected. A solid circle is a model detection. A dashed circle is a flight estimate across a short gap. Misses and false positives remain.

Estimated ball height

The opening serve and following shots (00:56.52–00:59.72) have an initial 3D flight fit. Bounce/contact times were manually reviewed; bounce height is constrained and contact heights are approximate priors. Gravity and linear drag fit the observed track. Height and velocity are estimates, not training ground truth. Other times have 2D tracking only or remain unknown. The 3D ball is enlarged for visibility.
